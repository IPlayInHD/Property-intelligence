## TRANSCRIPT 01
### Segment: Investor A — Enthusiastic, would pay
**Persona:** Ravi M., 44, Indian expat, IT director. Owns a 2BR in JVC and a studio in Business Bay. Bought both off-plan, one recently handed over.
**Interview date:** 10 June 2026 | **Duration:** 38 minutes | **Mode:** Video call

---

**1. Warm-up**

Been in Dubai eleven years. Bought the JVC unit in 2021 — it was still under construction. Business Bay studio I got in 2019, that one's been rented out since handover. Looking at a third one now, maybe Dubai Hills or Creek Harbour.

**2. Current Process**

*"Honestly it's a lot of tabs. A lot of tabs. I go to Bayut, Property Finder, then I manually check the DLD REST portal for recent transactions — which by the way takes forever to load — and then I'm on Facebook groups asking people who bought in the same building what they're getting for rent. It's embarrassing how unscientific it is. My JVC unit, I thought I was getting a great deal. Then I found out six months later someone on the same floor paid AED 80k less. Nobody told me that. The agent certainly didn't."*

He mentions spending 3-4 hours per property researching, often late at night. His wife jokes he has "Dubai property insomnia."

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

*"Wait — you're pulling from actual DLD transactions? Not just listing prices? That's... okay, that changes everything. Listing prices are fiction. Everyone knows that. If you're comparing against what actually registered at DLD, that's real data."*

[Pause]

*"The 5-year forecast — bear, base, bull — I love that framing. Because when I bought off-plan in JVC I had no idea I was buying at peak. A bear case would have made me think twice. Or at least size my position differently."*

**4. Most Valued Module**

Price Fairness Index without hesitation. *"That's the thing that would have saved me on JVC. I overpaid by probably AED 120k. I know that now."*

**5. Trust Objections**

Mild. Wants to know how frequently DLD data refreshes. *"If it's pulling transactions from six months ago, that's still useful but I need to know that. Transparency about data lag matters to me."*

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before pricing reveal:* "I'd pay maybe AED 50-60 a month if it's genuinely saving me money on decisions this size."
*After seeing pricing:* "AED 150 for Pro is nothing. I'm making decisions on AED 2-3 million assets. This is less than a coffee a day. Sign me up."

Immediately asked if there's an annual plan.

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
High-intent buyer. Emotional hook is the JVC overpayment — he's rationalising a past mistake and wants a tool that prevents recurrence. Data freshness is a real concern, not a deal-breaker. Annual billing likely converts well here. Follow up on: does he want portfolio tracking (multiple properties in one dashboard)?

## TRANSCRIPT 02
### Segment: Investor B — Sceptical about data accuracy
**Persona:** Fatima A., 51, Emirati, runs a small trading business in Deira. Owns 3 units — Arabian Ranches villa, Al Furjan apartment, one off-plan in Creek Harbour not yet handed over.
**Interview date:** 11 June 2026 | **Duration:** 42 minutes | **Mode:** In-person, her office

---

**1. Warm-up**

*"I've been buying property since 2008. I bought during the crash, which everyone thought was crazy. So I've seen the market go very up and very down. I don't trust people who say they can predict Dubai property."*

She laughs, but means it.

**2. Current Process**

Uses a property consultant she's known for twelve years — a Lebanese broker who also owns in the same communities. *"He knows the buildings. He knows the landlords. He knows which RERA registrations are fake and which are real. That knowledge isn't in any database."*

She also calls her property manager directly for vacancy data, and checks service charge notices from RERA herself. *"The service charges on my Al Furjan unit went up 22% last year. Nobody predicted that. My property manager didn't warn me. I found out when the bill came."*

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

*"DLD data. Okay. But DLD data includes bulk deals — developers selling to related parties, distressed sales, gifted property between family. Does your system clean that out? Because if it doesn't, the Price Fairness Index is meaningless."*

[Sceptical pause]

*"The Quality of Life score — who defines quality of life? For me it's walking to a good abaya shop and a majlis nearby. For a British family it's an international school and a Waitrose. These are not the same score."*

She's engaged but adversarial. She's testing the concept, not dismissing it.

**4. Most Valued Module**

True Rental Yield — but with conditions. *"If it actually accounts for real vacancy — not Dubai Statistics Centre vacancy, actual vacancy in that building — and if it deducts real service charges, not estimates, then yes. That number I'd trust. But it has to be granular. Building-level, not community-level."*

**5. Trust Objections**

Significant. Wants to understand the methodology behind DLD data cleaning, how Quality of Life weights are defined, and whether there's a way to customise assumptions. *"If I can put in my own service charge number, my own vacancy assumption, then I trust it more. Because then I'm the one responsible for the input."*

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before:* "I wouldn't pay for it without a free trial first. I need to see if the numbers match what I already know is true on properties I own."
*After pricing:* "AED 30 for the Analyst tier — I could try that. Run it against my Arabian Ranches villa, see if the yield number makes sense. If it does, maybe Pro."

She will not commit to Pro without validation.

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
Power user if trust is established — but the trust barrier is high. She's sophisticated enough to know the edge cases (bulk DLD deals, generic QoL scoring) and those are real product risks. The free trial / validation-against-known-property approach is a critical onboarding flow to design. Customisable input assumptions could be a Pro feature that directly addresses her objections. She represents the segment that will either become the loudest advocates or loudest critics.

## TRANSCRIPT 03
### Segment: Agent A — Sees it as a client tool, would pay Pro
**Persona:** Marcus T., 36, British expat, RERA-licensed agent at a mid-size brokerage in Dubai Marina. Specialises in secondary market sales in JLT, Dubai Marina, and JVC. 7 years in Dubai real estate.
**Interview date:** 12 June 2026 | **Duration:** 35 minutes | **Mode:** WhatsApp voice note + follow-up call

---

**1. Warm-up**

*"Seven years. Seen three cycles. The 2020 COVID dip, the 2022 spike, and whatever this is now. I do secondary market, no off-plan — I leave the off-plan commissions to people who like arguing with developers' sales reps."*

He's fast-talking, confident, clearly used to pitching.

**2. Current Process**

*"So when a client asks me whether a property is fairly priced, I genuinely have to build a manual CMA — comparative market analysis. I pull DLD transactions myself, usually via a subscription data tool that costs me AED 400 a month and is honestly terrible UX-wise. Then I put it in a spreadsheet. Then I send the client a PDF. It takes me maybe two hours per serious client, and half of them don't even make an offer."*

He estimates he does this for 8-10 clients a month. *"That's 16-20 hours of work that I'm doing for free, basically. Because it's part of closing."*

His biggest pain: clients who go cold after he's done all the analysis. And clients who find a listing on Bayut and ask "is this good?" and he has nothing quick to send them.

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

*"Right, so this is basically my CMA but automated. Okay."*

[Thinking]

*"Actually — the way I'd use this is I'd run it on every property I'm showing and just... send the report to the client before the viewing. Sets expectations, saves the negotiation conversation for after they've fallen in love with the kitchen. That's actually smart."*

He immediately starts thinking about workflow integration. *"Can clients run it themselves, or is it agent-facing?"*

**4. Most Valued Module**

Liquidity Score. *"Nobody talks about exit. Everyone talks about buying. I have clients sitting on JVC 1BRs they can't sell because the market's saturated. If I'd shown them a liquidity score two years ago, they might have bought differently — or I might have steered them differently. This is the thing that makes me look like I'm thinking about their whole journey."*

**5. Trust Objections**

Low for himself, moderate for clients. *"I'd trust DLD-backed numbers. Clients are going to ask me how it's calculated — I need to be able to explain it in thirty seconds. If the methodology is a black box, I can't use it with clients."*

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before:* "If it saves me two hours per client and makes me look smarter, I'd pay AED 200 a month easy."
*After pricing:* "AED 150 for Pro — unlimited analyses — yes, immediately. This replaces part of my AED 400/month data subscription and does more. I'd want to run it in parallel for a month to verify, but yes."

Already asked whether there's a white-label or co-branded report for agents.

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
Immediate convert for Pro. The use case reframe — send the report *before* the viewing — is a genuine insight that could be product positioning language. White-label report export is a high-signal feature request that multiple agents will likely share. The explainability requirement (methodology in 30 seconds) is a UI/UX challenge: methodology tooltips or a plain-English summary on each module. Consider an Agent plan tier with bulk client-sharing features.

## TRANSCRIPT 04
### Segment: Agent B — Worries it undermines their value
**Persona:** Nour S., 42, Lebanese expat, RERA-licensed senior agent at a large franchise brokerage. 11 years in Dubai, specialises in luxury secondary market — Palm Jumeirah, Emirates Hills, Jumeirah Bay. Average deal size AED 8M+.
**Interview date:** 13 June 2026 | **Duration:** 44 minutes | **Mode:** In-person, café in DIFC

---

**1. Warm-up**

*"I don't do volume. I did volume for four years and it nearly killed me. Now I do six, maybe eight deals a year. But those deals — my commission is real money. My clients are HNW, mostly European and GCC. They don't use Bayut. They call me."*

She's measured, well-dressed, orders sparkling water. Not rushed.

**2. Current Process**

Relationship-driven, not data-driven — by design. *"My value isn't the transaction data. Anyone can pull DLD. My value is I know which Palm villas have a sewage issue in the basement, I know which buildings on the fringe of the plot have construction noise that won't stop for three years, I know which developer is going to flip that parcel next to the community into a hotel. That's not in any database."*

She employs a part-time researcher who does transaction analysis for her. *"I pay him AED 4,000 a month. He does it well, and more importantly I trust him because I trained him."*

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

Long pause after the explanation.

*"So your platform gives a buyer everything I would normally give them, in a report, instantly."*

[Pause]

*"I see the threat. I'll be honest with you. If a buyer can pull a Price Fairness Index and a Forecast before they even call me, they come to the meeting already having done 'the analysis.' Then they think they don't need me. That's the story they tell themselves."*

She's not dismissive of the product. She's genuinely concerned about commoditisation. *"This is the same thing that happened to travel agents. First the tool helps them, then it replaces them."*

**4. Most Valued Module**

Conditionally: Neighbourhood Trends. *"If it shows hyperlocal data — not 'Palm Jumeirah' but 'Signature Villas vs Garden Homes vs Frond K' — then maybe I use it to confirm what I already know. That saves my researcher time. But it better be that granular. Because at my level, 'Palm Jumeirah' is useless. I need sub-community."*

**5. Trust Objections**

High, but the nature is different. She's not worried about data quality — she's worried about what the data *doesn't* capture. *"DLD data doesn't capture the off-market deals. And in my segment, probably 30% of transactions never touch DLD in a normal way. Gifted, related-party, structured differently. So your Price Fairness Index for a Palm villa could be badly skewed."*

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before:* "I wouldn't pay for it for myself. Maybe I'd pay for it to give access to clients — AED 20 a use, like a single analysis."
*After pricing:* "AED 30 a month I could try it personally, see if the neighbourhood trend data is actually useful at the sub-community level. But I'm not going Pro until I know it doesn't just make my clients think they've done my job for them."

She's protecting her model, not her time.

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
Hardest conversion — and potentially the most important to get right. Her travel agent analogy is sharp and worth taking seriously: PropIQ should position to agents as "makes your expertise look smarter" not "does the analysis for you." The off-market data gap in luxury is a real product blind spot for the Palm/Emirates Hills segment. Sub-community granularity is likely a hard technical requirement for luxury agent adoption. Consider a client-gifting model (agent buys credits, sends to clients branded from them).

## TRANSCRIPT 05
### Segment: First-Time Buyer A — Overwhelmed, finds PropIQ reassuring
**Persona:** Priya & Sanjay K., 32 and 34, Indian expats, both work in finance/tech. Married two years. Budget AED 1.8-2.2M, considering JVC, Jumeirah Village Triangle, or Dubai Hills.
**Interview date:** 14 June 2026 | **Duration:** 41 minutes | **Mode:** Video call, both on screen

---

**1. Warm-up**

*Priya: "We've been looking for eight months. Eight months! Every weekend is viewings. We've made two offers that didn't work out. It's exhausting."*

*Sanjay: "The first one, the agent told us it was a great price, we trusted him, then we found out the same floor plan sold for AED 180k less three months earlier. We felt stupid."*

**2. Current Process**

*Sanjay: "We have a spreadsheet. It's got 34 properties in it. We track price, size, price per square foot, which floor, view, agent contact. It took us probably... forty hours to build?"*

*Priya: "And it still doesn't tell us if we're being overcharged. We don't know. The DLD website is really hard to use. We tried. We gave up after two sessions."*

Their biggest fear: buying at the wrong price and being stuck. *"We can't afford to get this wrong. This isn't an investment for us, this is where we're going to live. Probably for ten years."*

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

*Priya: "Oh my god. You just paste the URL?"*

*Sanjay: "Wait, and it shows you the DLD transactions for that building automatically? That's exactly what we've been trying to do manually."*

*Priya: "The Quality of Life score — does it include schools? Because we're planning to start a family and I've been manually googling school ratings in JVC and it's all over the place."*

*Sanjay: "This would have saved us... months. Honestly. Months of weekends."*

**4. Most Valued Module**

Price Fairness Index immediately and jointly. Then Quality of Life Score. *"Schools and transport are our two biggest questions after price. If one report answers all three, we don't need to do anything else."*

**5. Trust Objections**

Mild. *"We just want to know the DLD data is current. If it's real transactions, we trust it more than any agent."* They've already had one bad agent experience — institutional data feels safer.

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before:* "We'd pay AED 50-100 for a one-time full report on the property we're about to buy."
*After pricing:* "AED 30 a month for Analyst — we'd do that for two or three months while we're still searching. That's AED 60-90 total, and we're making a AED 2M decision. That's nothing."

Asked whether there's a way to save and compare multiple analyses side by side.

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
High-emotion, high-intent. The "paste the URL" moment was the strongest positive reaction of the research. These users are doing enormous manual work (34-property spreadsheet) that PropIQ directly replaces. The comparison/save feature is a product priority — they're managing a shortlist, not evaluating one property. Key messaging: they don't trust agents after one bad experience; position PropIQ as the neutral, data-backed second opinion. The one-time report model they proposed is worth testing as a pricing alternative to subscription.

## TRANSCRIPT 06
### Segment: First-Time Buyer B — Would only use the free tier
**Persona:** Daniel O., 27, Nigerian expat, logistics operations. Single. Budget AED 700k-950k, considering International City, Liwan, or a studio in JVC.
**Interview date:** 15 June 2026 | **Duration:** 29 minutes | **Mode:** Phone call

---

**1. Warm-up**

*"I've been saving for this for four years. Four years. Every month I put money aside. So I cannot afford to make a mistake with it. This is my whole savings."*

Soft-spoken, deliberate. Has a spreadsheet of every studio sale in JVC for the last two years pulled from whatever free sources he could find.

**2. Current Process**

*"I use Bayut, Property Finder, the DLD REST thing — it's hard to use but I figured it out after a while. I also read the forums. There's a subreddit for Dubai property and some Facebook groups. People share a lot there."*

*"I don't want to pay someone to tell me something I can find out myself. Agents commission is already 2%, DLD fees are 4%, registration fees — by the time I've bought this thing it's AED 60,000 in fees just to own it. I have to be careful with every dirham."*

He knows JVC service charges run AED 12-15 per sqft on the buildings he's looking at.

**3. Concept Reveal Reaction**

*"So it pulls the DLD transactions automatically and compares them. Okay. That's what I'm trying to do manually. That would save me time."*

*"The Quality of Life score — I'm not worried about schools, I'm single. I care about metro access because I don't have a car. Is that in there?"*

[Told yes — transport is part of QoL score]

*"Okay. That matters to me. Bus and metro is how I get around. If a building is 20 minutes from a metro that's a real cost — taxi money every day."*

**4. Most Valued Module**

Quality of Life Score (transport specifically) and Price Fairness Index.

**5. Trust Objections**

Moderate. *"I'd want to cross-check it against what I already found manually. If it shows me a number that's different from what I found, I need to understand why. Not because I don't trust it — because I want to learn from it."*

**6. Willingness to Pay**

*Before:* "I wouldn't pay for it. Time is cheaper than money for me right now. I'd use a free version."
*After pricing:* "Five free analyses a month — that's probably enough. I'm looking at maybe ten properties total. I could spread it across two months."

[On AED 30 Analyst tier] *"Not right now. Maybe after I buy, if I want to buy a second property one day. But right now every AED counts."*

Unprompted: *"If I tell my friends who are also looking, that's worth something right? Do you have a referral thing?"*

---

**Interviewer Debrief Notes:**
Will not convert to paid — and that's fine. He's the free tier's core use case and will refer. The referral question was unprompted and important: a share/referral mechanic could drive organic acquisition in expat community networks. His transport-as-financial-cost framing is a compelling QoL use case not in current product copy — reframe QoL not just as lifestyle but as hidden cost of ownership. Note: International City and Liwan segments are underserved by most property data tools — worth checking DLD transaction density in those areas for Price Fairness Index reliability.
